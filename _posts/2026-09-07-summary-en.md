### [China’s Supreme Court Clarifies AI Liability](https://www.cnr.cn/news/20260907/t20260907_527806795.shtml) ⭐️ 8.0/10

China’s Supreme People’s Court issued a judicial interpretation on September 7 for artificial intelligence dispute cases, according to the source. The interpretation contains 5 parts and 24 articles covering AI face swapping, algorithmic price discrimination, impersonated endorsements, autonomous driving, privacy violations, and intellectual property issues. It states that using AI without consent to create identifiable faces, voices, or similar personal features may infringe personality rights, and that platforms or businesses can bear liability when algorithmic price discrimination harms user rights. It also says courts may support punitive damages where AI is used to impersonate another person’s endorsement to induce consumption, and it regulates AI-enabled “doxxing” and “human flesh search” activities that violate natural persons’ privacy rights.

**「Background」** In China, judicial interpretations issued by the Supreme People&\#x27;s Court guide lower courts on how to apply laws in specific categories of cases. The issues named here—AI face or voice synthesis, algorithmic price discrimination, impersonated endorsements, autonomous driving liability, privacy intrusions, and intellectual property—are areas where AI systems can create harms that existing civil, consumer-protection, privacy, and IP rules must be applied to concrete disputes.

**「Impact」** AI developers, platforms, advertisers, and consumer-facing businesses in China face clearer litigation risks around consent, personalization, endorsements, privacy, and pricing practices.
