### [Genome Models Generate Viable Phages](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vjj4pr/r_generative_design_of_novel_bacteriophages_with/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A Reddit post shares research claiming the first generative design of experimentally viable bacteriophage genomes using genome language models. The work reportedly used Evo 1 and Evo 2 to generate whole-genome sequences modeled on the lytic phage ΦX174, aiming for realistic genetic architectures and desirable host tropism. Experimental testing allegedly produced 16 viable AI-generated phages with substantial evolutionary novelty. If supported by the underlying study, the result would be significant for generative biology because it suggests language models can design functional biological systems at whole-genome scale, not just shorter sequences.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/moschles · Aug 9, 07:11

**「Background」** Bacteriophages are viruses that infect bacteria, and ΦX174 is a small, well-studied lytic phage often used as a reference system for genome-scale experiments. Genome language models such as Evo 1 and Evo 2 treat DNA sequences analogously to text, learning patterns that can be used to propose new complete genomes rather than only short genetic parts.

**「Impact」** For ML-for-biology researchers, the reported 16 viable AI-generated ΦX174-like phages provide experimental evidence that genome language models can design functional whole viral genomes, not just shorter biological sequences.
